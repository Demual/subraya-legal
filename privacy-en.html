Privacy Policy

Subraya app (com.jrsoft.lectorpdf) · Last updated: 4 November 2026

In one sentence: Subraya opens, reads and edits your PDFs inside your phone. There are no accounts and no servers of its own, and your documents, your annotations and your signatures never leave your phone unless you share or print them. Apart from that, the only data that leaves it is the technical and advertising data collected by Google's libraries to serve the ads, as explained below.

Who is responsible

The data controller is JR Soft, trading name of Javier Román Sáez, developer of the Subraya app. You can get in touch at demual@gmail.com.

What this app does NOT do

  • There is no sign-up, account, username or password for the app. You are never asked for an email address, a phone number, a name or your location.
  • It does not upload your documents. The PDFs you open, what you annotate, the forms you fill in and your signatures are processed and stored on your phone. This app has no servers of its own.
  • The app includes no analytics or crash-reporting tools of its own (no Google Analytics, no Firebase Analytics and no Crashlytics). Google's libraries do send their technical data, explained below.
  • We do not sell or rent your data. To show and measure ads, Google's ads SDK (AdMob) collects device identifiers, as explained in this policy. Some US privacy laws may treat that as “selling” or “sharing” personal data (see “US residents”).
  • It does not ask for permission to access your files or all of your storage, or to use the camera, your contacts or your location. PDFs are opened with Android's file picker or with “Open with” from another app, and the app can only read the ones you choose.
  • The app's data is not included in Android's backup to Google's cloud.

Permissions

  • Internet, network state and advertising identifier (AD_ID and the permissions of Android's advertising services), which the advertising and the Google libraries need (see below). Those libraries also add the technical permissions for their background tasks (WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE).
  • No others: no storage, camera, location or contacts permission. To open and save a PDF, Android gives the app access only to the file or folder you choose.

Data that stays only on your phone

This information is kept in the app's private storage. It disappears if you uninstall the app, if you clear its data from Android settings or if you use Settings → Delete all my data, which also gives up the access to your PDFs that the app had kept. Your PDFs are not deleted: they stay where they were.

  • The library of recent files and favorites: for each PDF, its name, its address on the phone (an Android content:// address), its size, its number of pages, its dates, the last page you read, whether it is a favorite and your bookmarks.
  • A thumbnail of the first page of each document, for the list.
  • The drawn signatures you save, as strokes.
  • Your settings: language, theme, reading mode, keep screen on and the other options, plus a few usage counters to know when to ask you for a rating.
  • Temporary copies of the PDFs you open, in the app's temporary storage (cache), because the PDF engine needs to read them there: the copies of the last 4 documents are kept, up to 600 MB in total, and Android can clear them whenever it wants. The copies you share or print are also prepared there.
  • While you save over the original, a safety copy of that PDF, which is deleted when saving finishes. If the app closes halfway through, it is kept so the app can offer to put the original back as it was.
  • The list of PDFs that froze the app, so they are not reopened automatically without asking you.
  • The permission to reopen each PDF in the library: when Android allows it, the app keeps the access you gave it to that file. It gives it up when you remove the PDF from the library and with Delete all my data.

Your annotations, forms and signatures go inside the PDF. What you highlight, underline, annotate or draw, the forms you fill in and the signatures you add are written into the PDF file itself when you save it, over the original or in a new PDF, as you choose. They travel with that file: whoever receives it will see them.

Backups and switching phones. Android's cloud backup (the one in your Google account) is turned off for this app's data: none of it is uploaded. Android does keep a list of the apps you had installed in your account so it can reinstall them, but Google manages that list and it contains no data from this app.

If, while setting up a new phone, you copy your data directly from the old one, with or without a cable, the app's data may be moved to the new phone. You ask for this transfer and Android carries it out, using its own tool or your phone maker's, under their own terms. According to Android's documentation, it goes from one phone to the other without going through servers. It never goes through ours: JR Soft receives nothing.

Whether it happens depends on your Android version and phone maker (it doesn't happen on Android 8 or earlier), so we can't guarantee it. If you set up the new phone from your Google account backup without the old phone, this app's data won't come back, because it isn't in that backup.

Anything that only makes sense on the old phone is not transferred, such as the temporary storage or your ad choices: the app will ask you again on the new phone.

What you choose to share

  • Sharing. A PDF only leaves your phone when you share it, with the app you choose on Android's screen, and it carries whatever you have annotated, filled in or signed. If you protect it with “Add password”, the PDF is encrypted with AES-256: if you forget the password, nobody can open it, not even us.
  • Saving. What you save goes over the original or into a new PDF, in the place you choose on Android's screen; “Split” puts its PDFs in the folder you choose. If that place syncs with a cloud (for example, Google Drive), the syncing is done by the service you chose, under its own terms.
  • Images to PDF. The app reads only the images you pick in Android's picker, at that moment, to make the PDF. It does not keep permission to read them again.
  • Printing. The PDF is sent to Android's print service and to the printer you choose, under their own terms. The copy the app prepares for printing is deleted when it finishes.
  • Links in a PDF. If you tap a web or email link inside a PDF, the app first shows you the full address and only opens it, in your browser or your email app, if you confirm.
  • Share the app. It shares, with the app you choose, a text with Subraya's link on Google Play. The link carries a parameter that only says it comes from “Share the app”, with no data about you.
  • Clipboard. The app copies text to the clipboard only when you ask it to.
  • Write to us. It opens your email app with a message addressed to us, with the subject “Subraya”. It is only sent if you send it. If you send it to us, we receive your email address and whatever you write; we use them only to reply to you and solve what you tell us about, and we delete the message once it is resolved and, at the latest, one year after receiving it.

Data that does leave your phone

Advertising: Google AdMob

The app is free and is supported by unobtrusive Google AdMob ads, which only appear in the library (a banner at the bottom and, now and then, one among the documents in the list): never while you read, annotate, fill in or sign a PDF, and never in full screen. Ad requests carry nothing from your documents.

As with any app that uses AdMob, Google's SDK collects the Android advertising identifier (you can reset or delete it in Android Settings → Google → Ads), the app set identifier (app set ID), your IP address (from which an approximate location is inferred), your interactions with the app and with the ads, and diagnostic data. Google uses this data to serve and measure advertising, for statistics and to prevent fraud, under its own policy.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the app shows Google's consent form before serving personalized advertising. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ad privacy options. If you decline, you will see non-personalized advertising (or limited advertising, without identifiers, if you also decline the use of device storage) and the app works exactly the same.

Google shows the ad consent message with wording that is common to all its advertising partners, which is why it mentions precise geolocation. According to Google, its ads SDK only uses the approximate location derived from your IP address, and this app does not give it your GPS location. You can refuse that use in “Manage options”.

How Google uses information from apps that use its services: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Ratings and Google Play

Every now and then, after you have used it successfully, the app may ask you for a rating through Google Play's official window, which is managed by Google. “Rate the app” and “More apps by JR Soft” open Google Play. To show you only the apps available in your country, “More apps by JR Soft” checks, on the phone, the country of the SIM card or of the system region; that information does not leave your phone. Its links to Google Play carry a parameter that only says they come from Subraya, with no data about you.

IP address

As with any app that connects to the internet, the Google services mentioned above receive your IP address when connecting. This app has no servers of its own, so it keeps no logs of any kind.

Legal basis and retention

Legal basis. What is processed on your phone does not reach JR Soft. For what does leave it: (1) personalized advertising and the use of device storage for advertising purposes in the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland: your consent, which you give or refuse in Google's form and can withdraw in Settings → Ad privacy options (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and Art. 22(2) LSSI-CE); (2) non-personalized advertising, its measurement and fraud prevention: the legitimate interest in funding a free app and protecting it from fraud (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); (3) if you write to us (for example with “Write to us”): the legitimate interest in replying to you and solving what you tell us about (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Google processes the AdMob data as an independent controller, under its own policies.

Data stored on your phone remains there until you delete it, uninstall the app or clear its data; the temporary copies, until the app replaces them with others or Android clears the temporary storage. This app keeps no data outside your phone, except the messages you send us, which are deleted once what you tell us about is resolved and, at the latest, one year after we receive them; Google keeps its own data according to its policies.

Your rights

If you live in the European Union, the GDPR and Spanish Organic Law 3/2018 (LOPDGDD) grant you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection. In practice, this app keeps none of your data outside your phone except the messages you send us (for those, write to the contact address); AdMob data is processed by Google, with whom you can exercise your rights (policies.google.com/privacy). For what is on your phone:

  • Access and portability: your PDFs are your own files, with what you annotate and sign inside them: share them or save them wherever you want. The library shows you what the app remembers about them.
  • Rectification: change your annotations, bookmarks, favorites and settings in the app itself.
  • Erasure: remove a PDF with “Remove from recent”, delete a signature with “Delete this signature” or use Settings → Delete all my data; you can also uninstall the app. There is no need to ask anyone.
  • Objection and withdrawal of consent: change your advertising choice in Settings → Ad privacy options.

You can also write to the contact address or lodge a complaint with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (Spain's authority) or with the data protection authority of your country.

US residents

If you live in a US state with its own privacy law (for example, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas or Virginia), you have the right to ask that your personal data not be sold or shared for targeted advertising. You can do this in the app: Settings → Ad privacy options → “Don't sell or share my data”. From then on, you will only see non-personalized ads. The app does not send sensitive data to anyone; your documents stay on your phone. For ads, Google's SDK uses device identifiers and an approximate location (city level). For any other request about your data, write to us at demual@gmail.com.

Residents of Brazil

If you live in Brazil, the General Data Protection Law (LGPD, Law 13,709/2018) gives you the rights of confirmation and access, correction, anonymization, blocking or deletion, portability, information about whom your data is shared with and about the possibility of not giving consent, and withdrawal of consent (art. 18). The legal bases are those described in “Legal basis and retention”: your consent for personalized advertising (art. 7, I) and legitimate interest for the rest (art. 7, IX). AdMob data may be processed outside Brazil, with the safeguards described in “International transfers” (art. 33). Your documents stay on your phone, and this app keeps none of your data outside it except the messages you send us; for any request, write to demual@gmail.com. You can also lodge a complaint with the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD).

Children

Subraya is not directed at children under 16 and we do not knowingly collect data from them. Ads are limited to content suitable for teenagers (rating T). If you are under 16 and live in the EEA, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, do not accept personalized advertising in Google's form (or withdraw it in Settings → Ad privacy options): the app works the same. The technical data sent by AdMob is the data described above.
